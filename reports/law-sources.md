# Law Sources: International Child Protection Laws Applicable to Online Gaming Platforms (Roblox Focus)

Annotated bibliography compiled via comprehensive literature search (Phase 1, Agent 1).
Topic: International child protection laws applicable to online gaming platforms, with
specific focus on Roblox. Jurisdictions covered: US, EU, UK, Australia, Brazil, UN/International,
South Korea, India. Recency weighting: 2020–present, with seminal older laws included.

---

## Laws & Statutes

### United States

1. **Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), 15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6505**
   - Authors / Organization: U.S. Congress / Federal Trade Commission
   - Year: 1998 (rule implemented 2000; amended 2013, 2025)
   - Source / Outlet: Federal Trade Commission
   - URL: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/childrens-online-privacy-protection-act
   - Relevance: Foundational US federal law governing data collection from children under 13; the baseline against which Roblox's age-gating and data practices are judged.
   - Key finding or provision: Requires operators of services directed to, or with actual knowledge of, users under 13 to provide notice and obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information; mandates data minimization and reasonable security.
   - Status: In force (original 1998 Act and 2013 Rule); 2025 amendments below.

2. **Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule, 16 CFR Part 312 — 2025 Amendments**
   - Authors / Organization: Federal Trade Commission
   - Year: Final rule published April 22, 2025; effective June 23, 2025; general compliance deadline April 22, 2026; Safe Harbor program compliance deadline October 22, 2025
   - Source / Outlet: Federal Register
   - URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule
   - Relevance: Most significant update to COPPA implementation in over a decade; directly affects how platforms like Roblox must handle children's data, including biometric identifiers (relevant to facial age-estimation tools).
   - Key finding or provision: (1) requires separate verifiable parental consent for disclosure of children's data to third parties for targeted advertising; (2) imposes new data retention limits (data must be deleted when no longer necessary); (3) expands the definition of "personal information" to include biometric identifiers (face templates, fingerprints, voiceprints), government IDs, and certain geolocation data.
   - Status: In force (effective June 23, 2025; phased compliance through October 2025–April 2026). Note: full text of 16 CFR Part 312 itself could not be directly fetched (eCFR/FTC pages returned redirects/403); summarized via Federal Register notice and secondary legal-industry analyses (Perkins Coie, White & Case, Mayer Brown, Davis Polk — all corroborating).

3. **California Age-Appropriate Design Code Act (CAADCA), Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1798.99.28–1798.99.40 (AB 2273)**
   - Authors / Organization: California Legislature
   - Year: Enacted 2022; intended effective date July 1, 2024
   - Source / Outlet: Court filings / legal commentary (Hunton Andrews Kurth, Alston & Bird, MoFo, Hintze Law)
   - URL: https://www.alstonprivacy.com/court-enjoins-enforcement-of-california-age-appropriate-design-code-act/
   - Relevance: Would impose UK-style "best interests of the child" design obligations (default high-privacy settings, restrictions on profiling, data minimization) on services likely accessed by minors — directly applicable to Roblox as a children's platform.
   - Key finding or provision: Requires Data Protection Impact Assessments, default privacy-protective settings for minors, and prohibitions on using children's data in ways materially detrimental to their wellbeing.
   - Status: **Enjoined / in litigation.** A second preliminary injunction was granted March 13, 2025 (N.D. Cal.) on First Amendment grounds (NetChoice v. Bonta); California AG appealed April 11, 2025. On March 12, 2026, the 9th Circuit overturned the injunction as to the definition of "covered businesses" but affirmed the injunction on five of six challenged provisions, remanding the age-estimation provision to the district court. **Conflict flag:** the law is simultaneously "enacted" and "blocked" — its core operative provisions are not currently enforceable.

4. **Texas SCOPE Act (HB 18, 2023) and 2025 App Store Accountability provisions**
   - Authors / Organization: Texas Legislature
   - Year: Enacted June 2023 (SCOPE Act); additional app-store age-verification statutes enacted 2025
   - Source / Outlet: Bass Berry & Sims; MultiState; Congress.gov CRS
   - URL: https://www.bassberry.com/news/apps-and-minors-new-compliance-frontiers-and-risks-in-louisiana-utah-and-texas/
   - Relevance: Texas is also pursuing direct litigation against Roblox (see Court Cases below) under related consumer-protection statutes, making this a live enforcement jurisdiction.
   - Key finding or provision: Requires parental consent via a "commercially reasonable method" for minors' accounts and restricts targeted advertising and data sharing involving minors; 2025 amendments extend obligations to app store providers (age verification, parental consent for downloads/purchases).
   - Status: Partially enjoined — a federal judge granted a preliminary injunction against the "harmful to minors" content provision in August 2024; other provisions and the 2025 app-store law remain in force/newly enacted.

5. **Utah Social Media Regulation Act (SB 152 / HB 311, 2023) and Utah App Store Accountability Act (2024)**
   - Authors / Organization: Utah Legislature
   - Year: Enacted March 2023 (Social Media Regulation Act); App Store Accountability Act 2024
   - Source / Outlet: Bass Berry & Sims; AVPA (Age Verification Providers Association)
   - URL: https://avpassociation.com/us-state-age-assurance-laws-for-social-media/
   - Relevance: Utah's app-store law is the first in the US to shift age-verification obligations to app store operators (Apple/Google) rather than individual platforms — a model with implications for how Roblox (distributed via app stores) would be age-gated.
   - Key finding or provision: Social Media Regulation Act required platforms with 5M+ accounts to verify ages of all account holders and obtain parental consent for minors; App Store Accountability Act requires app stores to verify user age and obtain verifiable parental consent before minors can download apps or make in-app purchases.
   - Status: Social Media Regulation Act **enjoined** (preliminary injunction granted September 2024, NetChoice litigation). App Store Accountability Act status: enacted 2024, implementation ongoing as of 2025.

6. **Louisiana Act 456 (2023) — Social media age verification / parental consent law**
   - Authors / Organization: Louisiana Legislature
   - Year: Enacted 2023
   - Source / Outlet: Louisiana Illuminator; WAFB; Congress.gov CRS
   - URL: https://lailluminator.com/2025/12/18/louisiana-social-media-2/
   - Relevance: Demonstrates the fragility of state-level age-verification mandates under First Amendment review — relevant precedent for assessing whether any future Roblox-specific state law would survive challenge.
   - Key finding or provision: Required social media companies to obtain parental consent before minors under 16 could create accounts.
   - Status: **Struck down.** A federal judge ruled the law unconstitutional (vague, violates free speech) in a December 2025 ruling.

### European Union

7. **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Regulation (EU) 2016/679 — Article 8**
   - Authors / Organization: European Parliament and Council
   - Year: 2016 (in force May 2018)
   - Source / Outlet: gdpr-info.eu (mirrors official EUR-Lex text)
   - URL: https://gdpr-info.eu/art-8-gdpr/
   - Relevance: The EU's core legal mechanism for regulating children's consent to data processing by information-society services, including gaming platforms — directly applicable to Roblox's EU operations.
   - Key finding or provision: Sets the default age of consent for information-society services at 16, with Member States permitted to lower it to no less than 13; requires controllers to make "reasonable efforts" to verify parental consent for children below the applicable age, "taking into consideration available technology."
   - Status: In force.
   - Note: Direct fetch of the EUR-Lex original returned an empty page; text verified via gdpr-info.eu, which mirrors the official Regulation text and is widely cited as authoritative by EU regulators (ICO, EDPB).

8. **Digital Services Act (DSA), Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 — Article 28**
   - Authors / Organization: European Parliament and Council
   - Year: 2022 (Article 28 obligations applicable from February 2024)
   - Source / Outlet: eu-digital-services-act.com (unofficial consolidated text); European Commission guidelines (see Regulations section)
   - URL: https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_28.html
   - Relevance: Imposes a free-standing "high level of privacy, safety and security" obligation on platforms accessible to minors, and bans profiling-based advertising to known minors — squarely applicable to Roblox as an online platform with a predominantly under-18 EU user base.
   - Key finding or provision: Article 28(1) requires "appropriate and proportionate measures" to protect minors; Article 28(2) bans presenting profiling-based advertisements to users the platform is aware (with reasonable certainty) are minors; Article 28(3) clarifies that compliance with Article 28 does not require platforms to collect *additional* personal data to determine user age.
   - Status: In force (applicable since February 17, 2024 for all platforms; from 2023 for Very Large Online Platforms).
   - Note: Direct EUR-Lex fetch returned empty content; text corroborated across European Commission, Hogan Lovells, Freshfields, and AEPD (Spanish DPA) summaries, which are consistent on Article 28(3)'s data-minimisation carve-out.

### United Kingdom

9. **UK Age Appropriate Design Code ("Children's Code")**
   - Authors / Organization: Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), under Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR
   - Year: Came into force September 2020 (full enforcement from September 2021)
   - Source / Outlet: ICO
   - URL: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/
   - Relevance: The most directly applicable UK framework to Roblox's product design — covers any online service "likely to be accessed by children," including games rated PEGI 16 or lower.
   - Key finding or provision: 15 standards including: high-privacy default settings; data minimisation; geolocation off by default; no "nudge techniques" toward lower privacy; profiling off by default unless justified.
   - Status: In force; ICO has published gaming-specific compliance guidance (2023) recommending privacy-protective defaults and natural play "checkpoints."

10. **UK Online Safety Act 2023**
    - Authors / Organization: UK Parliament
    - Year: Royal Assent October 26, 2023; provisions phased in through 2024–2025
    - Source / Outlet: legislation.gov.uk
    - URL: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50
    - Relevance: Imposes statutory "child safety duties" on user-to-user services likely accessed by children — Ofcom has explicitly stated gaming platforms with chat/UGC functionality (i.e., Roblox) fall within scope.
    - Key finding or provision: Requires children's risk assessments; mandates "highly effective" age verification or age estimation to prevent children encountering primary priority content (e.g., pornography, self-harm/suicide content); requires protective measures embedded in service design, algorithms, and moderation; grants Ofcom investigatory and enforcement powers including fines.
    - Status: In force (Royal Assent Oct 2023); Ofcom's Protection of Children Codes of Practice came into force April 2025 (see Regulations section).

### Australia

11. **Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth)**
    - Authors / Organization: Parliament of Australia
    - Year: 2021
    - Source / Outlet: Federal Register of Legislation
    - URL: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2021A00076/latest
    - Relevance: Establishes the eSafety Commissioner and the general online-safety regulatory architecture that the 2024 social media minimum-age amendment (below) sits within.
    - Key finding or provision: Empowers eSafety Commissioner to issue removal notices for harmful content and sets "Basic Online Safety Expectations" for industry; age-assurance is required only for content classified R18+ (gaming platforms generally excluded from age-check requirements unless hosting R18+ content).
    - Status: In force.

12. **Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024 (Cth), No. 127, 2024**
    - Authors / Organization: Parliament of Australia
    - Year: Passed November 29, 2024; came into effect December 10, 2025
    - Source / Outlet: Federal Register of Legislation
    - URL: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00127/asmade
    - Relevance: World-first ban on under-16 social media accounts; **gaming platforms, including Roblox, are explicitly excluded from the definition of "age-restricted social media platform"** — a key regulatory gap directly relevant to Roblox's classification.
    - Key finding or provision: Requires "age-restricted social media platforms" (Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit, X, Threads, Twitch, Kick) to take "reasonable steps" to prevent under-16s from holding accounts, with penalties up to A$49.5 million; online gaming services are carved out of scope based on the statutory definition and policy rationale (avoiding regulatory overlap with classification scheme).
    - Status: In force as of December 10, 2025. Early implementation reports (NPR, CNBC) note significant circumvention by minors.

### Brazil

13. **Estatuto Digital da Criança e do Adolescente ("Digital ECA")**
    - Authors / Organization: Brazilian National Congress
    - Year: Enacted September 17, 2025; entered into force March 17, 2026
    - Source / Outlet: Agência Brasil; Baker McKenzie; Human Rights Watch
    - URL: https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/en/politica/noticia/2026-03/brazil-digital-statute-protect-minors-online-comes-force
    - Relevance: One of the most stringent and recent frameworks globally; explicitly covers "electronic games" and prohibits self-declared age — directly responsive to Roblox's current age-gating model.
    - Key finding or provision: Prohibits simple self-declaration of age for account creation; requires linking under-16 accounts to a legal guardian; mandates removal of content involving sexual exploitation, violence, or self-harm within 24 hours; bans profiling-based advertising and certain immersive (AR/VR/emotion-recognition) advertising techniques targeting minors; enforcement by ANPD (data protection authority) with fines up to 10% of Brazilian revenue or BRL 50 million (~US$9.4M) per violation.
    - Status: In force as of March 17, 2026.

### International / UN

14. **UN Convention on the Rights of the Child — General Comment No. 25 (2021) on children's rights in relation to the digital environment**
    - Authors / Organization: UN Committee on the Rights of the Child
    - Year: Adopted 2021 (86th session)
    - Source / Outlet: UN Digital Library / 5Rights Foundation
    - URL: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/3906061?ln=en
    - Relevance: The authoritative international interpretation of how the UNCRC's binding obligations (ratified by all UN member states except the US) apply to digital products, including games — provides the normative baseline ("best interests of the child," "evolving capacities") against which national laws and platform design are increasingly judged.
    - Key finding or provision: States parties must ensure that businesses providing digital products/services likely to be accessed by children undertake child-rights due diligence, including child-rights impact assessments; reaffirms non-discrimination, best interests, and evolving capacities as principles applicable to "design, regulation and management" of the digital environment.
    - Status: Authoritative interpretive guidance (not binding treaty text itself, but interprets binding UNCRC obligations). Note: produced via consultation with 700+ children across 27 countries.

### Other Jurisdictions

15. **India — Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025**
    - Authors / Organization: Government of India / Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology
    - Year: Act passed 2023; Rules notified 2025; substantive children's-data obligations enforceable from May 13, 2027
    - Source / Outlet: K&S Partners; EY India; Mondaq
    - URL: https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/childrens-data-protection-under-indias-dpdp-rules/
    - Relevance: Defines "child" as under 18 (broader than COPPA's under-13), creating a stricter standard that would apply to a much larger share of Roblox's user base in India than COPPA does in the US.
    - Key finding or provision: Requires *verifiable* parental consent (via OTP, ID upload, or Aadhaar-based authentication) for processing any data of users under 18; restricts behavioural tracking, profiling, and targeted advertising directed at children; penalties up to ₹200 crore (~US$24M).
    - Status: Act in force; Rules notified 2025; children's-data provisions enforceable from May 13, 2027 (compliance grace period).

16. **South Korea — Juvenile Protection Act; Game Industry Promotion Act (loot box disclosure); E-Commerce Act amendment 2025 (dark patterns)**
    - Authors / Organization: National Assembly of the Republic of Korea / Korea Fair Trade Commission / Game Rating and Administration Committee (GRAC)
    - Year: Loot box disclosure requirements in force; E-Commerce Act dark-patterns amendment effective February 14, 2025
    - Source / Outlet: Chambers and Partners Gaming Law 2025 — South Korea
    - URL: https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/gaming-law-2025/south-korea
    - Relevance: South Korea is the most prescriptive jurisdiction reviewed on monetisation mechanics — directly comparable to the Robux/loot-box allegations against Roblox.
    - Key finding or provision: Monthly spending caps (KRW 70,000 / ~US$60 for under-18s); mandatory transparent disclosure of loot box probabilities; 2025 E-Commerce Act amendment specifically targets "dark patterns" in digital commerce; Juvenile Protection Act requires age and real-name verification for content designated "harmful to juveniles."
    - Status: In force.

## Regulations & Guidance

17. **European Commission — Guidelines on the protection of minors under Article 28(1) DSA**
    - Authors / Organization: European Commission (Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology)
    - Year: Published July 14, 2025
    - Source / Outlet: Shaping Europe's Digital Future (European Commission)
    - URL: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors
    - Relevance: The most detailed regulatory articulation to date of what "appropriate and proportionate measures" under DSA Article 28 mean in practice — provides a checklist against which Roblox's chat, virtual currency, and recommender-system features can be assessed.
    - Key finding or provision: Recommends private-by-default minor accounts, restrictions on stranger contact, modified recommender systems, disabling addictive design features (streaks, autoplay, push notifications) by default, prohibiting screenshotting/downloading of minors' content, and explicitly flags "virtual currencies and loot boxes" as commercial practices that can exploit children's lack of commercial literacy.
    - Status: Non-binding (guidelines), but Commission states it will use them as the benchmark for Article 28(1) compliance assessments and enforcement.

18. **EDPB Guidelines 3/2025 on the interplay between the DSA and the GDPR**
    - Authors / Organization: European Data Protection Board
    - Year: 2025 (published for consultation)
    - Source / Outlet: edpb.europa.eu
    - URL: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-09/edpb_guidelines_202503_interplay-dsa-gdpr_v1_en.pdf
    - Relevance: Directly addresses the "age assurance paradox" — the tension between DSA Article 28's safety mandate and GDPR's data-minimisation principle — central to the Gaps analysis required for this report.
    - Key finding or provision: EDPB recommends platforms avoid age-assurance mechanisms that enable "unambiguous online identification" of users; favours privacy-preserving approaches such as selective disclosure and "double-blind" age verification (neither platform nor verifier can link identity to platform activity).
    - Status: Guidance document (non-binding but highly influential on national DPA enforcement).

19. **Ofcom — Protection of Children Codes of Practice (under Online Safety Act 2023)**
    - Authors / Organization: Ofcom (UK communications regulator)
    - Year: Published April 24, 2025; in force from July 2025
    - Source / Outlet: Ofcom
    - URL: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/new-rules-for-a-safer-generation-of-children-online
    - Relevance: Operationalises the Online Safety Act's child-safety duties into 40+ specific measures applicable to "social media, search and gaming" — the binding compliance reference for Roblox's UK operations.
    - Key finding or provision: Requires platforms to use "highly effective age assurance" to identify child users and apply protective settings (safer algorithms, restricted contact from strangers, content warnings) by default for child accounts; specific guidance issued for online video game providers covering chat, avatars, matchmaking, and livestreaming risks (grooming, harassment, harmful content).
    - Status: In force.

20. **ICO — Guidance on Children's Code compliance for game developers**
    - Authors / Organization: Information Commissioner's Office (UK)
    - Year: 2023
    - Source / Outlet: ICO / RPC Lexology summary
    - URL: https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=56103890-5c97-4e6b-8429-a637a7d83a6e
    - Relevance: Sector-specific application of the Children's Code to game design choices (in-game economies, chat, push notifications) most analogous to Roblox's product surface.
    - Key finding or provision: Recommends defaulting to high-privacy settings, parental control tools, and natural breaks/checkpoints in gameplay design; clarifies that any game with a PEGI rating of 16 or below falls within the Code's scope.
    - Status: Active guidance.

21. **eSafety Commissioner (Australia) — Basic Online Safety Expectations & Age-Restricted Material Codes**
    - Authors / Organization: eSafety Commissioner (Australia)
    - Year: Codes reached full commencement 2025
    - Source / Outlet: eSafety Commissioner
    - URL: https://www.esafety.gov.au/industry/basic-online-safety-expectations
    - Relevance: Sets baseline industry-wide expectations (separate from the under-16 social media ban) that would apply to Roblox regardless of its exclusion from the "age-restricted social media platform" definition.
    - Key finding or provision: Sets "reasonable steps" expectations for providers regarding minimising harmful content (violence, pornography, self-harm/suicide material) reaching children, applicable across "device manufacturers to app stores, social media and websites."
    - Status: In force.

## Court Cases & Enforcement Actions

22. **FTC v. Epic Games, Inc.**
    - Authors / Organization: Federal Trade Commission
    - Year: Settlement announced December 2022; finalized March 2023
    - Source / Outlet: FTC press release
    - URL: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/12/fortnite-video-game-maker-epic-games-pay-more-half-billion-dollars-over-ftc-allegations
    - Relevance: The largest COPPA enforcement action to date and the clearest precedent for how US regulators view default-on chat features and "dark pattern" virtual-currency purchase flows in games — both central allegations against Roblox.
    - Key finding or provision: $275M civil penalty (largest COPPA penalty ever) for collecting children's data without parental consent and enabling default-on voice/text chat exposing children to harassment; separate $245M consumer-redress order for dark patterns that tricked users (including children) into unintended purchases via in-game currency (V-Bucks).
    - Status: Settled; consent order in force, requiring privacy-protective defaults.

23. **FTC v. Microsoft Corporation (Xbox)**
    - Authors / Organization: Federal Trade Commission
    - Year: 2023
    - Source / Outlet: FTC press release
    - URL: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2023/06/20-million-ftc-settlement-addresses-microsoft-xbox-illegal-collection-kids-data-game-changer-coppa
    - Relevance: Establishes that account-creation flows which collect full birthdate/contact data before any parental-consent check violate COPPA — relevant to Roblox's self-declared birthdate signup process.
    - Key finding or provision: $20M settlement; Microsoft collected children's personal information (name, email, full DOB) during Xbox sign-up without adequate notice or verifiable parental consent; ~218,000 under-13 accounts affected.
    - Status: Settled.

24. **FTC v. Cognosphere Pte. Ltd. (Genshin Impact / HoYoverse)**
    - Authors / Organization: Federal Trade Commission
    - Year: January 2025
    - Source / Outlet: FTC / law firm summaries (KH Law)
    - URL: https://www.khlaw.com/insights/privacy-and-advertising-year-review-2024-will-kids-and-teens-remain-focus-2025
    - Relevance: Most recent (2025) FTC gaming enforcement; demonstrates continued regulatory focus on virtual-currency monetisation of minors and undisclosed loot-box-style odds — directly parallel to Robux allegations.
    - Key finding or provision: $20M settlement for COPPA violations and deceptive marketing (obscured costs of in-game purchases and odds of obtaining items); company required to block under-16 users from making in-game purchases without parental consent.
    - Status: Settled.

25. **FTC v. The Walt Disney Company**
    - Authors / Organization: Federal Trade Commission
    - Year: September 2025
    - Source / Outlet: FTC enforcement summaries
    - URL: https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/ftc-starts-2025-with-a-focus-on-safeguarding-kids/
    - Relevance: Shows FTC's expanding theory of "actual knowledge" liability (platform-level knowledge of child-directed content) — a theory potentially applicable to UGC platforms like Roblox hosting child-directed "experiences."
    - Key finding or provision: $10M penalty for COPPA violations arising from child-directed YouTube content mislabeled "Not Made for Kids," resulting in data collection from children without parental consent.
    - Status: Settled.

26. **National Center on Sexual Exploitation & Fairplay — FTC Complaint against Roblox**
    - Authors / Organization: National Center on Sexual Exploitation (NCOSE) and Fairplay
    - Year: Filed May 20, 2026
    - Source / Outlet: The Hill; Claims Journal; CBS News Atlanta
    - URL: https://thehill.com/homenews/5888060-roblox-gaming-platform-ftc-investigation/
    - Relevance: The most direct and recent enforcement-track input specific to Roblox's regulatory exposure; frames the platform's monetisation and chat features as a coordinated COPPA/UDAP issue rather than isolated incidents.
    - Key finding or provision: Alleges Roblox's game mechanics coerce children into excessive spending via an obfuscated Robux exchange rate, and that its "engagement-maximizing design features, virtual currency system, and voice/text chat" are "developmentally inappropriate" for its young user base, posing risk of sexual and financial harm.
    - Status: Complaint filed; FTC investigation outcome pending as of report date (June 2026).

27. **Louisiana v. Roblox Corporation**
    - Authors / Organization: Office of the Louisiana Attorney General (Liz Murrill)
    - Year: Filed August 2025
    - Source / Outlet: NBC News
    - URL: https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/louisiana-attorney-general-sues-roblox-calling-platform-perfect-place-rcna225144
    - Relevance: First state AG suit against Roblox specifically; establishes the factual and legal template (no effective age verification, fake-birthdate accounts, named harmful "experiences") used by subsequent state suits.
    - Key finding or provision: Alleges Roblox "intentionally or recklessly" designed a platform with no real age-verification process, allowing millions of accounts with fake birthdays; cites specific harmful user-generated "experiences" (e.g., "Escape to Epstein Island," "Diddy Party") as evidence of moderation failure.
    - Status: Active litigation; consolidated into MDL 3166 (see below).

28. **Texas v. Roblox Corporation**
    - Authors / Organization: Office of the Texas Attorney General (Ken Paxton)
    - Year: Filed November 7, 2025
    - Source / Outlet: Texas Tribune; TechCrunch
    - URL: https://www.texastribune.org/2025/11/07/texas-roblox-lawsuit-ken-paxton/
    - Relevance: Third state AG suit; relies on Texas's 2023 SCOPE Act consumer-protection provisions, providing a state-statutory (not just common-law) basis for liability.
    - Key finding or provision: Alleges Roblox exposed children to sexually explicit content and exploitation; relies on 2023 Texas legislation requiring social platforms to safeguard minors (the same provisions previously used against TikTok).
    - Status: Active litigation; third state to sue after Kentucky and Louisiana. Florida and Oklahoma have since filed similar suits (per The Hill, May 2026).

29. **In re: Roblox Corporation Child Sexual Exploitation and Assault Litigation, MDL No. 3166**
    - Authors / Organization: U.S. Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation
    - Year: Consolidation order December 12, 2025; assigned to N.D. Cal., Chief Judge Richard Seeborg
    - Source / Outlet: Top Class Actions; Levin Law; PBG Law
    - URL: https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/lawsuit-news/roblox-child-sexual-exploitation-lawsuits-consolidated-in-california-mdl/
    - Relevance: Represents the consolidated federal civil-litigation exposure for Roblox (separate from the state AG suits) and signals that courts view these as "systemic, platform-wide" design issues rather than isolated user misconduct — relevant to the Section 230 / design-liability gap analysis.
    - Key finding or provision: Initially consolidated 31 federal actions from 12 districts (162 cases as of June 2026); JPML found common questions include Roblox's alleged knowledge that predators used the platform, alleged misrepresentations to parents about safety, feasibility of parental controls, and failure-to-warn. A settlement master (Thomas J. Perrelli) was appointed in February 2026.
    - Status: Active; settlement negotiations ongoing as of report date.

30. **NetChoice v. Bonta (California AADC litigation)**
    - Authors / Organization: U.S. District Court, N.D. Cal.; Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals
    - Year: Second preliminary injunction March 13, 2025; 9th Circuit ruling March 12, 2026
    - Source / Outlet: Hintze Law; Morrison Foerster
    - URL: https://hintzelaw.com/blog/2026/3/17/9th-circuit-rules-on-injunction-of-california-age-appropriate-design-code-upholding-coverage-definition
    - Relevance: Establishes the First Amendment vulnerability of US "design code" approaches to child online safety — important for assessing whether similar Roblox-targeted design mandates would survive challenge in the US (vs. UK/EU where no equivalent free-speech doctrine constrains such codes).
    - Key finding or provision: District court found CAADCA's content-moderation/risk-assessment provisions likely unconstitutional as content-based speech regulation failing strict scrutiny; 9th Circuit (March 2026) reinstated the law's definition of "covered businesses" but upheld the injunction on five of six challenged provisions, remanding the age-estimation requirement specifically.
    - Status: Active; age-estimation provision remanded to district court.

31. **Massachusetts v. Meta Platforms, Inc. (Section 230 ruling)**
    - Authors / Organization: Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court
    - Year: Ruling April 10, 2026
    - Source / Outlet: Crowell & Moring
